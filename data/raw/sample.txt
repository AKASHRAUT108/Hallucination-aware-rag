Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) combines a retriever with a language model. The retriever searches an external document collection for passages relevant to a question, and the language model uses those passages as context when writing its answer. This grounds the output in real documents instead of relying only on what the model memorised during training. Even so, RAG systems can still hallucinate when the retrieved passages are incomplete, ambiguous, or only partly relevant. A hallucination-aware system adds a verification step that checks whether each claim in the answer is supported by the retrieved context, and lowers its confidence or refuses to answer when support is weak.
